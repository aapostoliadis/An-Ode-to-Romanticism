How the paintings come alive

The Carrières des Lumières shows (Culturespaces, AMIEX process) project thousands of digitised artworks across 7,000 m² of quarry walls, pillars and floor, set in motion and cut to a soundtrack. This program rebuilds that vocabulary in real time on the GPU.

  1. Strokes that move. The direction of every brushstroke is measured from the image, and the paint is advected along it with a looping flow map, so skies and seas churn the way Turner painted them.
  2. Depth without cut-outs. A pseudo depth map from aerial perspective drives 2.5D parallax as the camera glides and the pointer moves.
  3. Slow camera. Each painting gets a long push toward its focal point and an easing pull back.
  4. Living elements. Water ripples below the horizon, mist drifts, smoke and steam rise, fire flickers, embers, rain and snow fall or spiral in the vortex.
  5. Light. A breathing glow and god rays from the light source, pulsing with the music.
  6. The painting paints itself. Colour washes first, then the strokes, spreading out from the light.
  7. Transitions. Brushstroke wipes, watercolour bleeds, floods of light, melting paint, mosaics.
  8. The space. Quarry view wraps the frame around a limestone hall with pillars and visitors.
